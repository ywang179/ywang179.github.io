03Decision #1For sales and customers

Text first, call only if needed.

To add a customer on WeChat, sales first had to reach her by phone, often after 40+ calls, because most people in China ignore unknown numbers as spam.

Before40+ calls to reach one customer

Sales sideType every number by handI don’t like wasting time repeatedly pressing call. It’s frustrating.
Customer sideAn unknown number, so no one picks upWhen I see an out-of-city number, I usually ignore it, assuming it’s spam.

From first lead to signed trip.

From one call to the right trip

Her past chats and an AI summary show what the traveler needs, so sales can suggest the right trip and next step.

Key numbers, a funnel with action buttons and today's calendar turn the data into the day's to do list.

—What I Learned

The team needed context too, not just the sales.

1. Write down the why. I kept a short note on the reason for each decision. When engineers or new teammates asked, the note answered, so we never had the same debate twice.

2. Test assumptions early. Mine weren't always right, so I listened back to call recordings, with transcripts and AI summaries, to check them before designing.

3. Small steps beat one perfect design. We tried four toolbar layouts in short, time boxed rounds, which brought in feedback early and kept brainstorms focused.

Thank you to my mentor and product lead, Liu Yu, who taught me the business and how to think like a product person through feedback on my daily reports and patient answers to my questions. The team had no designer then, so I got to be both PM and designer, which made this internship especially valuable.
